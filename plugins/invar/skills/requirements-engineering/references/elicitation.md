# Requirements Elicitation

## Objective

Discover the actual problem, stakeholder needs, desired outcomes, and constraints without prematurely choosing a solution.

## Procedure

1. Read the request and applicable project instructions, existing SPEC.md, relevant decisions, and current behavior. Reuse SA intent findings where available; do not repeat technical discovery. Use architecture and code as evidence of the current system, not automatic evidence of desired behavior.
2. Identify who experiences the problem, who benefits from the outcome, who is affected, and who can authorize decisions. Do not invent stakeholder positions.
3. Separate the problem, goal, explicit requirements, proposed solutions, constraints, and non-goals. A user-prescribed technology may be a real constraint; do not dismiss it as merely a suggestion without evidence.
4. Investigate relevant usage scenarios: triggers, actors, preconditions, successful outcomes, failure conditions, and environmental constraints.
5. Identify implicit needs as hypotheses. Distinguish observed facts, direct statements, inference, and unknowns. Check for conflicts with existing approved requirements.
6. Ask only questions whose answers materially affect behavior, obligations, acceptance, or authorization. Present concrete alternatives and consequences where helpful. Do not block independent work unnecessarily.

## Evidence record

For substantial tasks, capture the problem, goal, stakeholder/source, explicit needs, candidate inferred needs, applicable constraints, non-goals, and open questions. Record source references where available. Do not create a separate document if the existing specification or plan is sufficient.

## Exit criteria

The intended outcome and relevant evidence are understood sufficiently for analysis. Material unknowns are explicit; unresolved questions are not represented as approved decisions.


## Handoff
Use [the shared contract](../../scope-management/references/requirements-and-scope.md). Record results in the Requirements Baseline or its proposed revision; report material unknowns without asserting authorization.
